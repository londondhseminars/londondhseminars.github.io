# London Digital Humanities Seminars brand guide

## Typography

**Space Grotesk** is the display typeface for logo lockups, banners, headings, navigation, and other titles. Its geometric forms give the identity a clear, contemporary voice while remaining readable at seminar-list and document sizes.

**Inter** is the supporting typeface for body copy, descriptions, metadata, and longer explanatory text. It keeps dense information calm and legible beside the more distinctive display face.

The website includes local Space Grotesk font files so the identity does not depend on a third-party font service. The SVG lockups and banners reference those same files for consistent standalone viewing and rasterisation.

## Colour palette

- **Ink** `#0D1B2A`: primary text, outlines, and dark surfaces.
- **Slate** `#475569`: secondary text and supporting information.
- **Light** `#F2F4F7`: pale page surfaces.
- **Red** `#E32017`
- **Orange** `#F4A61E`
- **Green** `#2A9D8F`
- **Blue** `#0077B6`
- **Purple** `#6A4C93`
- **Pink** `#E76F9C`
- **Yellow** `#F4D35E`

The neutral colours carry the page while the bright colours identify routes, nodes, links, and active states. They are used as a distributed system rather than as a single dominant wash.

## Visual language

The identity is built from nodes and edges: a network of distinct points connected by different methodological routes. The circular nodes suggest people, ideas, and moments of encounter; the coloured edges suggest movement between disciplines and approaches.

The visual grammar has a distinctly London feel through its reference to Underground diagrams: clear routes, strong colour coding, and a sense of a city made legible by connections. It also belongs to Digital Humanities because the network is non-hierarchical and methodological: no single centre, object, or discipline has to govern the whole field.

The resulting tone is editorial, precise, and open. Generous space and strong typographic hierarchy keep the system usable for websites, seminar announcements, and the top of an A4 document.
